The Black Hole
A black hole is not really a hole at all, but a region where matter has been packed so tightly that not even light can climb back out. Around its edge, called the event horizon, time itself bends, slowing down for anything brave or unlucky enough to drift close. At the heart of nearly every large galaxy, including our own, there sits a giant one, calmly steering the orbits of countless stars. We took our first picture of one only recently, and it looked exactly like a small, perfect ring of fire around a circle of impossible black.
