The Blackout
When the grid went down, the megacity fell silent in a way it had not been silent in sixty years. No ads, no drones, no humming towers. People came out onto balconies and stared up at a sky they had only ever seen in old photographs, crowded with stars so sharp it almost hurt. Strangers passed candles across the walkways. Someone on the ninetieth floor began to play a violin. By morning the power returned, and nobody could explain why they felt so sad about it.
