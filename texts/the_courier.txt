The Courier
Jax rode a matte black bike with no lights and a memory chip sewn into the lining of her jacket. Thirty thousand credits for twelve minutes of work, which meant somebody wanted the data badly and somebody else wanted it gone. She cut through the market, ducked under a freight elevator, and took the canal path where the cameras had been blind for years. Behind her a quadcopter whined, searching. She didn't look back. Looking back was how couriers got caught.
